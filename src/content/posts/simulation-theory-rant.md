Let me be quick and short here.

You must have heard of the [Olbers Paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olbers%27_paradox), (or not) which states that the sky should be completely white if the universe was infinite. One answer to that paradox was that the speed of light is a constant, and that light from some stars are yet to reach us because the universe is expanding and so on and so forth. 

Let's look at a hypothetical scenario where we've created a simulation for a smaller version of our world and want to stop the inhibitants from looking at places where we don't want them to, like an incomplete area. Viola! constant speed of light.

This constant prevents us from looking into (or outwards) the universe after a specific distance. I am by no means an expert at this. I'm but a humble Sri Lankan guy who is learning to write blog articles. This will be my first proper article.
